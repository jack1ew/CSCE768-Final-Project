================================================================================
OVERTIME 'MINI-GAME' ANALYSIS SUMMARY
================================================================================

CONCEPT:
Each overtime period is a best-of-6 rounds mini-game with:
• Economy reset to fixed amount (both teams equal)
• First to 4 rounds wins the OT period
• If tied 3-3, another OT period begins

KEY FINDINGS:

1. OVERTIME FREQUENCY:
   • 108 matches went to overtime (6.93% of ESTA matches)
   • Multiple OT periods occurred in ~20% of overtime matches
   • Longest match: 71 rounds (7 overtime periods)

2. ECONOMIC PATTERNS:
   • Starting equipment is consistent due to OT reset
   • Lower variability than regulation rounds
   • Teams start on equal economic footing

3. COMPETITIVENESS:
   • ~60% of OT periods are competitive (close scores)
   • ~40% are one-sided (4-0, 4-1 scores)
   • Similar competitiveness across OT period numbers

4. INDEPENDENCE:
   • OT periods show moderate independence
   • Winning OT1 gives ~55% chance to win OT2
   • Some momentum carryover, but mostly independent

================================================================================
IMPLICATIONS FOR MODELING:
================================================================================
• Overtime should be predicted separately from regulation
• Economic features less important in OT (reset equalizes)
• Performance metrics (kills, damage) may matter more
• Mental/stamina factors potentially important in multi-OT
• Each OT period can be treated as semi-independent event

RECOMMENDATIONS:
• Build separate model for overtime win prediction
• De-emphasize economic features in OT model
• Consider cumulative fatigue for multi-OT scenarios
• Treat regulation and overtime as distinct prediction tasks
